Inspired by Karpathy's LLM Wiki pattern

Hear the strongest case for both sides.

Adversarial Wiki compiles two opposing knowledge bases for any topic, makes each argue its best case, then names the hidden assumption each side relies on and the other never challenges.

Install the CLISee a debate

The output is a map, not a verdict.

Every debate runs three model calls: the pro wiki argues, the con wiki argues, and a third call finds what each side quietly assumes. Then it asks you the questions that would actually settle it.

Question

Should our engineering team go fully remote?

Loaded 4 pro article(s), 3 con article(s).
[Call 1/3] Pro side arguing… [Call 2/3] Con side arguing… [Call 3/3] Surfacing hidden assumptions…
Wiki A argues

Remote work gives engineers longer unbroken focus blocks and fewer interruptions, per [[async-communication]]. [[productivity-research]] shows teams ship more when they control their environment.

Wiki B argues

The best engineering work comes from unplanned collaboration. [[knowledge-transfer]] shows onboarding slows sharply when tacit knowledge stops flowing, and [[team-cohesion]] ties in-person time to trust.

Wiki A assumes

Your bottleneck is individual focus, and your team already communicates well in writing.

Wiki B assumes

Your value comes from emergent collaboration, maybe because the team is junior or the product needs tight coordination.

Before you decide, answer these
  1. What is your team's tenure mix?
  2. Is the product in maintenance or active exploration?
  3. Is your real bottleneck focus time, or shared understanding?

Your answers feed the next round of sharper questions. Every round is appended to the output file.

Compile. Debate. Deepen.

Each wiki is a folder of linked concept pages with an index, so retrieval reads the index first and loads only the relevant articles.

01

Compile two wikis

Manual mode reads your .md, .txt and .url files from raw/pro and raw/con. Auto mode writes search queries per stance and fetches sources itself.

02

Debate a question

Both sides argue from their own articles, citing them as [[wiki-links]]. A third call surfaces each side's core assumption.

03

Deepen your thinking

An interactive loop turns your answers into progressively targeted questions until you type stop.

Try it · Python 3.11+

Two modes, one command line.

Manual mode
You choose the evidence. Drop sources into each side's raw folder and compile.
Auto mode
The tool researches the web per stance. Set your own --pro and --con stances or let it choose.
Lint
Health checks before you trust a wiki.
# install
git clone https://github.com/shivamshinde123/adversarial-llm-wiki.git
cd adversarial-llm-wiki && pip install -e .
# .env → ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=…

# research both sides
adversarial-wiki compile --topic "remote-work" --auto \
  --pro "remote work boosts productivity" \
  --con "onsite work strengthens culture"

# argue it out
adversarial-wiki debate --topic "remote-work" \
  --question "Should our team go fully remote?"

# check wiki health
adversarial-wiki lint --topic "remote-work"

What lint checks, on both sides.

  • Each wiki folder exists and is not empty
  • Each side has an index.md
  • No orphaned concept pages missing from the index
  • No broken [[wiki-links]] in any page
  • Auto mode: sources.json is intact
  • Auto mode: every page has valid frontmatter
